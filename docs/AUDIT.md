# ArcticEdge Ski-Metric Audit

Generated by a parallel find/verify subagent audit (62 agents: 7 finder dimensions, adversarial verification per finding, plus a completeness critic). 4 critical, 13 high, 16 medium, 19 low survived verification (2 refuted). Every critical was re-checked by hand against the source.

Goal of the audit: confirm the ski-metric pipeline is suited to a pocket-worn iPhone and produces accurate, useful data for a skier.

## Verdict

Blunt verdict: not yet fit for accurate ski-metric tracking on a pocket iPhone, and the audit's 50+ findings, while strong on the DSP and concurrency internals, under-cover two whole classes of blocker that decide whether the app produces any trustworthy data at all. Class one is capture reliability at the iOS boundary: capture dies on screen-lock (no UIBackgroundModes, already flagged), but on top of that location authorization is NEVER requested, reduced/approximate location is never detected, the whole day hard-depends on a HealthKit workout session that can deny or fail and abort all capture, and 100 Hz frames accumulate forever with swallowed save errors so a cold-weather shutdown or full disk silently loses data. Class two is metric trustworthiness: the genuinely good part is the gravity-referenced ScoringFrame design (verticalAccel, horizontalAccelMagnitude, yawRateAboutVertical, angularSpeed are honestly pocket-safe and orientation-robust, and the geometric-mean anti-gaming aggregation is sound), but the GPS-gated pillars run on a speed channel quantized to one stale scalar per multi-second batch while reporting ~100% coverage, the vertical/pressure numbers are physically meaningless (already flagged), and crucially the score's anchors are frozen with no wired recalibration and no per-device/per-pocket normalization, so no two runs, skiers, or phones are comparable. The biggest blockers to the product promise, in order: (1) background capture plus the entire location/HealthKit authorization lifecycle, (2) the batch-scalar, mistimed GPS path feeding the intensity pillar and its coverage gate, (3) calibratability and cross-run/cross-device comparability of the headline number, and (4) cold-weather and crash data-loss integrity. Until (1) and (4) hold, the app often collects nothing; until (2) and (3) hold, the number it shows is not comparable enough to be the 'headline feature.' Keep the gravity-projection core; treat everything touching GPS, authorization, persistence durability, and anchor calibration as unproven.

### One unstructured Task per sample destroys frame ordering and corrupts the IIR filter state
`ArcticEdge/Motion/MotionManager.swift:82` · concurrency · motion-signal-integrity · verdict: confirmed (high confidence)

- **Problem:** Each CoreMotion callback spawns a fresh top-level `Task { await self.ingest(...) }`. Independent Tasks have NO ordering guarantee: they start on the concurrent global executor and each then hops to the actor, so the order in which they arrive at MotionManager's mailbox is nondeterministic, regardless of the order CoreMotion delivered the frames. The 2nd-order biquad is a strictly sequential IIR: `filter.apply()` mutates an internal delay line that assumes samples arrive in time order. Any reordering feeds samples to the filter out of sequence, poisoning the delay memory for many subsequent outputs, and also lands out-of-order frames (by timestamp) into the RingBuffer.
- **Why it matters:** filteredAccelZ is the headline 'carve pressure' signal and the primary input to the provisional carving score (persisted to FrameRecord, exported by CalibrationExporter, drawn in LiveTelemetryView). Even occasional reordering at 100 Hz produces garbage filtered output, so the score and live waveform are computed on a corrupted signal that cannot be trusted for calibration.

The invariant to guarantee: filter.apply() is called exactly once per sample, in timestamp order.

### SPARC and LDLJ-A computed over the whole run span, not per turn as designed, giving a strong run-length bias
`ArcticEdge/Scoring/CarvingScorer.swift:52` · data-accuracy · carving-score-math · verdict: confirmed (high confidence)

- **Problem:** The design of record (docs/CARVING-SCORE.md:42-43) specifies SPARC on |rotationRate| 'per turn' and LDLJ-A as a per-turn smoothness metric. The implementation instead computes each ONCE over the entire concatenated turn span: `edgeSmoothRaw = SignalMath.sparc(slice(angularSpeed, span), ...)` (line 51) and `linkageRaw = SignalMath.logDimensionlessJerk(acceleration: slice(horizontal, span), ...)` (line 52), where `span = turnSpan(turns, gridCount)` runs from the first turn's start to the last turn's end (line 48, 222-226). LDLJ-A's normalization only removes duration dependence for a single sub-movement; over an N-turn signal it does not. For a periodic accel A*sin(wt) the raw LDLJ = -ln((duration/peak^2)*integral) = -2*ln(T) - ln(w^2/2), so raw LDLJ falls by ~2*ln(2)=1.39 for every doubling of run length. Against the frozen anchor span (bad -22, good -4, width 18) a 4x-longer run of IDENTICAL technique loses ~2.77 raw = ~15% of the normalized scale purely from duration.
- **Why it matters:** The frozen model exists specifically so scores are comparable across a season. A length-dependent linkage metric means a long groomer run scores systematically lower than a short one for the same skier, which is exactly the cross-run comparison the product promises. The existing test cleanRunOutscoresJerkyRun uses two 14 s runs (equal length), so the bias is invisible to the suite.
- **Fix:** Compute SPARC and LDLJ-A per turn (slice each turn from startIndex..<endIndex), then aggregate with a robust mean (e.g. trimmedMean, which already exists) across turns. This matches the design, removes the run-length bias, and makes each metric a true per-turn quality average. Add a regression test with two runs of the same technique but very different durations and assert their linkage sub-scores are within a small tolerance.
